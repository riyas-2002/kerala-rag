# Kerala Business Regulations — Key Acts and Rules

## 1. Factories Act, 1948

**Scope**: Governs working conditions in factories across India, including Kerala.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 2(m): Definition of "Factory" — 10+ workers with power, 20+ without power
- Chapter III: Health requirements — cleanliness (Sec 11), ventilation (Sec 13), lighting (Sec 17), drinking water (Sec 18), latrines (Sec 19)
- Chapter IV: Safety requirements — fencing of machinery (Sec 21), hoists and lifts (Sec 28), fire exits (Sec 38)
- Chapter V: Welfare — washing facilities (Sec 42), first-aid (Sec 45), canteens (Sec 46), crèches (Sec 48)
- Chapter VI: Working hours — max 48 hrs/week (Sec 51), rest interval (Sec 55), weekly holiday (Sec 52)
- Chapter VII: Employment of young persons — no child below 14 (Sec 67)
- Chapter VIII: Annual leave with wages (Sec 79)

**Kerala-specific Rules**: Kerala Factories Rules, 1957

---

## 2. Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006

**Scope**: Regulates food safety standards throughout India, including Kerala.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 25-26: FSSAI licensing and registration system
- Section 27: Prohibition on manufacture, sale or import of unsafe food
- Section 29: Food recall procedures
- Section 38-40: Powers of Food Safety Officers — inspection, sampling, seizure
- Schedule I–V: Standards for various categories of food

**Penalties**:
- Selling substandard food: Fine up to ₹5 lakh
- Misbranded food: Fine up to ₹3 lakh
- Unsafe food causing grievous hurt: Imprisonment up to 7 years + fine up to ₹10 lakh

---

## 3. Kerala Municipality Act, 1994

**Scope**: Governs functions of municipalities, including trade licensing.

**Relevant Sections**:
- Section 411: Power to regulate offensive and dangerous trades
- Section 413: Grant of license for dangerous trades
- Section 417: Penalty for operating without license

**Kerala Panchayat Raj Act, 1994**: Equivalent provisions for panchayat areas.

---

## 4. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

**Scope**: Prevents water pollution by industries.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 25: Prohibition on discharging polluting matter without consent from PCB
- Section 26: Applies to existing discharges — must obtain consent
- Section 33: Power of Board to restrain apprehended pollution

**Penalty**: Imprisonment 1.5–6 years + fine (Section 44)

---

## 5. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

**Scope**: Controls air pollution from industries.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 21: No person shall establish any industrial plant in an air pollution control area without PCB consent
- Section 22: Consent required to operate an industrial plant
- Section 22A: PCB may direct closure of defaulting industries

**Penalty**: Imprisonment up to 6 years + fine

---

## 6. Environment Protection Act, 1986

**Scope**: Umbrella legislation for environmental protection in India.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 5: Power to issue directions — including closure, prohibition, or regulation of industry
- Environment Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification, 2006: Mandatory EIA for large projects

**EIA Thresholds relevant for Kerala**:
- Construction projects > 20,000 sqm built-up area: EIA required (Category B)
- Industrial estates > 500 ha: Category A EIA
- Mining > 5 ha: Category B
- Hotels > 100 rooms in ecologically sensitive areas: EIA required

---

## 7. Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1960

**Scope**: Regulates employment conditions in shops and commercial establishments in Kerala.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 7: Establishment must be registered before opening
- Section 10: Working hours — max 9 hours/day, 48 hours/week
- Section 11: Weekly holiday — every employee entitled to 1 day rest
- Section 15: Overtime — double wages for overtime work
- Section 19: Leave — earned leave at 1 day per 20 days worked
- Section 21: Maternity leave as per Maternity Benefit Act
- Section 27: Prohibition on employment of children

---

## 8. Kerala Panchayat Building Rules, 2011 & Kerala Municipality Building Rules, 2019

**Scope**: Govern construction and land use in Kerala's local body areas.

**Key Aspects**:
- Floor Space Index (FSI): Varies by location and land use zone
- Setbacks: Minimum distances from plot boundary
- Building Height Limits: Depends on road width and zone
- Heritage Zone Restrictions: Special rules near heritage sites
- Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ): Construction prohibited within 500m of High Tide Line (HTL) in ecologically sensitive areas

---

## 9. Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006

**Scope**: Defines and promotes MSMEs in India.

**Key Provisions**:
- Section 7: Definition of micro, small, and medium enterprises (as amended in 2020)
- Section 11: Priority sector lending obligations on banks for MSME credit
- Section 15-24: Provisions for timely payment — buyer must pay MSME within 45 days (15 days if no agreement); MSME can recover 3x bank rate as compound interest on delayed payments
- Section 31: National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC) benefits

**MSMED Act 2006 — Revised Size Criteria (2020)**:
| Category | Investment | Turnover |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Micro | ≤ ₹1 crore | ≤ ₹5 crore |
| Small | ≤ ₹10 crore | ≤ ₹50 crore |
| Medium | ≤ ₹50 crore | ≤ ₹250 crore |

---

## 10. Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970

**Scope**: Regulates employment of contract labour in establishments.

**Applicability**: Establishments employing 20+ contract workers; contractors employing 20+ workers.

**Key Provisions**:
- Principal employer must obtain Registration Certificate
- Contractor must obtain Licence
- Contractor must provide amenities: canteen, rest rooms, first-aid
- Principal employer responsible if contractor fails to pay wages

---

## 11. The Building and Other Construction Workers (BOCW) Act, 1996

**Scope**: Welfare of construction workers.

**Applicability**: Construction projects costing ₹10 lakh or more.

**Key Requirements**:
- Register establishment with Labour Department
- Pay BOCW cess at 1% of construction cost
- Provide safety equipment, first-aid, accommodation (if residential project)

---

## 12. Kerala Preservation of Trees Act, 1986

**Scope**: Protects trees in Kerala. Highly relevant for construction projects.

**Key Provisions**:
- Permission required from local Tree Officer to cut any tree on private property
- Certain species are protected: teak, rosewood, rubber, cashew, coconut, etc.
- Coconut tree felling: Must obtain permission from panchayat/municipality
- Penalty for illegal felling: Fine + imprisonment

**Note**: This is a frequently overlooked compliance for construction and land development projects in Kerala.

## Important Kerala-Specific Regulations

### Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) — MoEFCC
- CRZ-I: Ecologically sensitive areas — no development
- CRZ-II: Developed coastal areas — regulated development
- CRZ-III: Undeveloped rural coastal areas
- CRZ-IV: Water area up to 12 nautical miles
- **Kerala backwaters and coastal strips fall under CRZ** — check zone before project

### Kerala Tourism (Conservation and Preservation of Areas) Act, 2005
- Establishes Responsible Tourism zones
- Eco-tourism projects in forest buffer zones need Forest Department clearance

### Kerala Land Utilisation Order, 1967
- Agricultural land cannot be converted without permission from Revenue Department
- Form 42 application for land conversion
